> Backdated to November 22, when I first wrote this idea down.

I reopened Blender recently. For a long time I didn't have a clear project to anchor my practice, so this time I picked something concrete to work on seriously: modeling a convenience store.

Whenever I picture Tokyo streets, a corner konbini is usually the first thing that comes to mind. The lit sign, the shelves and fluorescent lights visible behind the glass doors—even an ordinary street corner instantly carries that everyday atmosphere once it's in frame. At first I just wanted a familiar subject with a fairly regular structure: walls, doors, windows, and all the small fixtures inside seemed like a good way to practice and get comfortable with Blender's basics again.

As I worked on it, a bigger idea crept in: since I'm already starting with a convenience store, maybe I could eventually build a Tokyo walking simulator.

In my head it wouldn't need complicated mechanics or systems. Just being able to stroll down a quiet Tokyo street, pass a glowing convenience store, intersections, and residential blocks, take in the scenery, and listen to ambient street sounds—capturing that feeling of walking through Japan already sounds appealing enough.

Stepping back, though, my current skill level is still a long way from shipping an actual playable game. I haven't finished a full project in Blender before, and making even a single convenience store look convincing already takes a lot of detail work. Turning that into a whole street later would mean tackling materials, lighting, scene optimization, and getting everything running interactively inside a game engine. With where my skills are right now, I honestly don't know if I can bring the scene in my head to life.

Luckily, this is a personal side project with no hard deadline. I'll start by finishing this one convenience store bit by bit, looking things up when I get stuck and building up the fundamentals. A "Tokyo walking simulator" is still far off, but I'll keep working on my skills at my own pace. When there's real progress to share, I'll write the next update here.
